About

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the Andlinger Center for Energy and the Environment at Princeton University. My research bridges three scientific communities — environmental engineering, integrated energy system modeling, and decision-making under deep uncertainty (DMDU) — to advance health-centered energy transitions under uncertainty.

My work pursues two interconnected themes. First, I connect energy transitions with public health by developing integrated energy–air quality–health modeling frameworks to understand how decarbonization pathways shape air pollution and health outcomes. Second, I explore scenario ensembles to identify robust energy and environmental strategies across deep uncertain futures.

I received my Ph.D. in Environmental Engineering from The Pennsylvania State University (2026), with time as a trailing Ph.D. student at Princeton's School of Public and International Affairs (2024–2026). I hold an M.Sc. from Imperial College London (2022) and a B.Sc. from Nanjing University (2021).
